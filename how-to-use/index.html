Why it works this way the memory science, kept brief

1. Testing beats re-reading (Active Recall)

Highlighting notes feels productive, but your brain barely works. Pulling an answer out of memory — even if you get it wrong first time and read the correction — builds far stronger recall.

Why you see quick checks, missions and flashcards everywhere.

2. Two different days beat cramming (Spacing)

Getting 20 questions right in one Sunday-night session feels easy, then fades by next week. Waiting a day lets your memory slightly fade, so recalling it again locks it in long-term.

Why homework is 1 short mission on 2 different days — and why spec points need 2 days to turn green.

3. Catching the "I know this" trap (Calibration)

It's easy to recognise a diagram and think you know it, then freeze in the exam. Comparing your confidence against proved quiz scores spots blind spots early.

Why the Spec Tracker flags points you rated "Confident" but haven't proved yet.

4. Mixing topics makes memory stick (Interleaving)

Practising one easy topic over and over feels smooth, but exams jump between topics. Mixing due cards and questions forces your brain to choose the right method each time.

Why Flashcards has a "Study all due (mixed)" button.

How a spec point turns green ("Secured")

→
3. Secured ✓ (Day 2+)Got it right first time on a second day. Locked in (and boosts your Questing stats!).

Tip: Flashcards rated Good or Easy on two different days count for up to half of securing a spec point — quiz questions in Practice or Questing prove the rest. After 3 weeks without practice, a point turns Rusty as a gentle reminder to refresh it.
