B1

D1
Circular muscles behind the bolus contract (squeezing the gut narrower) while muscles in front relax, pushing the bolus forward in waves.
D2
Fibre is not digested, giving gut muscles solid bulk to grip and push against. Peristalsis uses muscular waves, not gravity, so astronauts can still swallow in space.
Don’t confuse egestion (removing undigested faeces) with excretion (removing metabolic waste made inside cells). Peristalsis uses circular and longitudinal muscles; ingestion = taking food in; assimilation = cells using absorbed food.

E2
Large molecules are insoluble and too big to pass through the gut wall; digestion breaks them into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood.
E3
1. Kills bacteria in food. 2. Provides the low (acidic) optimum pH for stomach protease (pepsin).
F1–F2
F1: liver · gall bladder · duodenum (small intestine). F2: emulsification.
F3
1. Bile is alkaline, so it neutralises stomach acid to give the optimum pH for lipase/small-intestine enzymes. 2. It emulsifies large lipid drops into tiny droplets, giving a much larger surface area for lipase to work faster.
F4
Bile is NOT an enzyme and does NOT break lipid molecules into fatty acids and glycerol. It physically emulsifies lipids into tiny droplets; lipase is the enzyme that digests them.
Examiners love catching students calling bile an enzyme — never do!

G & H  Absorption and coeliac disease

G1–G3
P: epithelium (one cell thick). Q: blood capillary (absorbs glucose & amino acids). R: lacteal (absorbs fatty acids & glycerol).
G4
Long/villi/microvilli → huge surface area · 1-cell-thick epithelium → short diffusion distance · rich capillaries → carry molecules away to keep a steep concentration gradient · lacteal → transports absorbed lipids.
H1
Flattened villi give a much smaller surface area in the small intestine → far less glucose and amino acids absorbed into the blood → less glucose reaches cells for respiration (feeling tired) and less nutrient absorption for growth/storage (weight loss).
Link structural change (flattened villi) → smaller surface area → less absorption → less glucose for respiration.
Villus adaptations (4 key ideas): microvilli (huge surface area), wall one cell thick (short diffusion distance), dense capillary network (maintains steep gradient), and lacteal (absorbs fatty acids and glycerol).
