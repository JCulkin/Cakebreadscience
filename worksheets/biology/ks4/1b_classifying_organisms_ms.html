Watch out for A: students often say "plant" because it has chloroplasts, but plants are multicellular — a single cell with a nucleus and chloroplasts (like Chlorella) is a protoctist.

B  Borderline organisms: Euglena and yeast

B1. Group
protoctist
B2. Why not a plant or an animal?
Not a plant: single-celled (plants are multicellular) / swims with a flagellum / takes in food in the dark.
Not an animal: single-celled (animals are multicellular) / has chloroplasts and photosynthesises.
has both plant-like and animal-like features, and is single-celled
B3. Yeast vs bacteria and Mucor
Eukaryote (not a bacterium): yeast has a nucleus (and mitochondria / membrane-bound organelles), whereas bacteria have no nucleus.
Shares with Mucor (fungi): chitin cell wall, stores carbohydrate as glycogen, saprotrophic nutrition (no chloroplasts).
nucleus (eukaryote) · chitin wall / glycogen / no chloroplasts (fungus)
Don't confuse single-celled with prokaryotic: yeast and protoctists are single-celled eukaryotes.

C  How fungi feed: saprotrophic nutrition

C1. Order of steps
1. Hyphae grow over and into the bread.
2. Hyphae secrete digestive enzymes onto the bread.
3. Enzymes break large insoluble food molecules into small soluble ones outside the fungus.
4. Small, soluble molecules are absorbed into the hyphae.
numbers down the table: 4, 1, 3, 2
C2. Why extracellular digestion?
Enzymes are secreted outside the cells onto the food, where large molecules are broken down into small soluble ones before being absorbed.
digestion happens outside the cells, before absorption
Extra- means outside (compare extranuclear, extraterrestrial).

D  Scale and microscopes

D1. Conversion and order
0.5 mm × 1000 = 500 μm
500 μm  ·  virus, bacterium, yeast cell, Amoeba
D2. How many times longer?
5 μm ÷ 0.1 μm = 50
50 times
Convert to the same unit before comparing or dividing (1 mm = 1000 μm).
D3. Light microscope limit
The virus (0.1 μm) is smaller than the 0.2 μm limit of a light microscope, so it cannot be resolved (an electron microscope is needed).
the virus (0.1 μm < 0.2 μm)

E  Pathogens and viruses

E1. Tobacco mosaic virus
Fewer chloroplasts → less light absorbed → less photosynthesis → less glucose made → less glucose for respiration and making new cells/biomass, so slower growth.
fewer chloroplasts → less photosynthesis → less glucose → less growth
E2. Is a virus alive?
Disagree: viruses can only reproduce inside living host cells (using the host's machinery), not on their own. They have no cells and do not carry out the other life processes (nutrition, respiration, excretion, growth, sensitivity/response).
no — not cellular, cannot carry out MRS C GREN independently
State both why the student's point is limited (only inside host cells) and which other life processes are missing.

bacterium: Pneumococcus (causes pneumonia)  ·  protoctist: Plasmodium (causes malaria)  ·  virus: influenza virus / HIV / tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)
