B  The menstrual cycle

B1
X oestrogen · Y progesterone
B2
about day 14 (just after the oestrogen peak)
B3
Oestrogen rises in the first half of the cycle and makes the uterus lining thicken / repair after the period. Progesterone rises after ovulation and keeps the lining thick. When progesterone falls, the lining breaks down (menstruation).
Don't say progesterone builds the lining: that's oestrogen.
Key terms: ovulation = egg release (~day 14); menstruation = shedding the uterus lining. High progesterone inhibits further ovulation and maintains the lining during pregnancy.

C  Fertilisation to embryo

C1
The nucleus of the sperm fuses with the nucleus of the egg (fertilisation), making a zygote. The zygote divides by mitosis many times into a ball of cells, the embryo, which implants in the uterus lining.
"The sperm enters the egg" alone is not enough: the nuclei fuse.

D  The placenta and amniotic fluid

D1
oxygen, glucose and amino acids: mother → embryo · carbon dioxide, urea: embryo → mother
D2
By diffusion between the two blood supplies, which are very close together in the placenta.
D3
The embryo floats in it; the fluid cushions it against bumps (a shock absorber).
The mother’s and baby’s blood do not mix in the placenta — substances cross by diffusion across thin walls. The umbilical cord carries blood between foetus and placenta; the amnion holds the amniotic fluid.
