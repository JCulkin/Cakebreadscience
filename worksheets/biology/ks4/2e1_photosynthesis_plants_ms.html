C  Limiting factors

C1
light intensity
Increasing light increases the rate, so light is limiting.
C2
At Y, light is no longer limiting — something else (CO₂) is. Raising CO₂ from 0.04% to 0.4% increases the maximum rate, proving CO₂ was the limiting factor at Y.
C3
10→32 °C: particles/enzymes have more kinetic energy, so more frequent successful collisions. Above 35 °C: enzymes denature (active site changes shape, substrate no longer fits), so rate drops to zero.
Never say enzymes are "killed" — they are proteins, not living things; say denatured.
C4
Lamps increase light intensity; burning paraffin increases both temperature and CO₂ concentration → faster photosynthesis → more glucose → more growth/tomatoes. At 45 °C enzymes would denature and photosynthesis would stop.

F  Oxygen from pondweed

F1
IV: distance from lamp (light intensity). Controls: temperature, CO₂ concentration, same piece of pondweed, time counted.
F2
NaHCO₃ supplies plenty of CO₂ (so CO₂ is not limiting). The beaker of water absorbs heat from the lamp to keep temperature constant.
F3
As distance increases, light intensity decreases and bubbles/min decrease. Test for oxygen: a glowing splint relights.
F4
Bubbles are different sizes (or hard to count when fast). Better: collect the gas in an inverted measuring cylinder or gas syringe and measure volume in a set time.

G  Mineral ions

magnesium ions (Mg2+)chlorophyllyellow leaves (chlorosis), poor growth

G2
Less magnesium → less chlorophyll made (leaves look yellow) → less light absorbed → less photosynthesis → less glucose made → less respiration and fewer molecules for making new cells, so growth is slow.
Always link mineral deficiency through the full chain: ion → molecule → photosynthesis/proteins → growth.
