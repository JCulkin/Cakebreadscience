A  Comparing waves

(a) λ = v ÷ f, so λA = (3v) ÷ (2f) = 1.5 × λB = 1.5 × 6.0 = 9.0 cm
(b) The wavelength halves. Because f = 1 ÷ T, halving T doubles f; v is unchanged, so λ = v ÷ f halves (to 3.0 cm).
(a) 9.0 cm  ·  (b) halves (3.0 cm)
Work with ratios rather than inventing numbers for v and f.

B  Satellite TV

(a) f = 12 GHz = 1.2 × 10¹⁰ Hz; λ = v ÷ f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.2 × 10¹⁰ = 0.025 m = 2.5 cm
(b) 3.6 × 10⁴ km = 3.6 × 10⁷ m; distance up and back down = 2 × 3.6 × 10⁷ = 7.2 × 10⁷ m
    t = d ÷ v = 7.2 × 10⁷ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 0.24 s
(c) The question goes up and down (0.24 s), and the reply goes up and down again (0.24 s): about 0.48 s in total.
(a) 2.5 cm  ·  (b) 0.24 s  ·  (c) two return trips ≈ 0.48 s
km → m: multiply by 1000, so 3.6 × 10⁴ km = 3.6 × 10⁷ m.

C  Oscilloscope trace

(a) 2.5 waves fill 10 divisions, so 1 wave = 4 divisions; T = 4 × 2 ms = 8 ms = 0.008 s
(b) f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ 0.008 = 125 Hz
(c) λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 125 = 2.72 m (2.7 m to 2 s.f.)
(d) Frequency doubles, so time period halves: 4 ÷ 2 = 2 divisions.
(a) 0.008 s  ·  (b) 125 Hz  ·  (c) 2.7 m (2.72 m)  ·  (d) 2 divisions
Count divisions across several complete waves, then divide — more accurate than reading one wave by eye.

D  Approaching storm

(a) d = v × t = 340 × 4.5 = 1530 m
(b) t = 1530 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 5.1 × 10⁻⁶ s. This is tiny compared with 4.5 s (and human reaction time), so the light effectively arrives instantly.
(c) New distance = 340 × 3.0 = 1020 m; storm moved 1530 − 1020 = 510 m in 2 min (120 s)
    speed = 510 ÷ 120 = 4.25 m/s (4.3 m/s to 2 s.f.)
(a) 1530 m  ·  (b) 5.1 × 10⁻⁶ s  ·  (c) 4.3 m/s (4.25 m/s)
Convert 2 minutes to 120 s. Dividing by 2 gives 255 m/s — always sense-check whether a storm moves at jet speed!

E  Ultrasound scan

(a) f = 2.0 MHz = 2.0 × 10⁶ Hz; λ = v ÷ f = 1540 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁶ = 7.7 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.77 mm
(b) t = 65 μs = 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ s; total distance = 1540 × 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.100 m (there and back)
    depth = 0.100 ÷ 2 = 0.050 m = 5.0 cm
(c) Number of waves = f × t = 2.0 × 10⁶ × 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ = 5 waves
    pulse length = 5 × 0.77 mm = 3.85 mm (3.9 mm to 2 s.f.)
(a) 0.77 mm  ·  (b) 5.0 cm  ·  (c) 5 waves, 3.9 mm (3.85 mm)
μ (micro) = × 10⁻⁶. Forgetting to halve an echo distance gives double the real depth (10 cm).

F  Locating an earthquake

(a) Time for S-waves − time for P-waves = 50 s:
    d ÷ 4.5 − d ÷ 8.0 = 50
    d × (0.2222 − 0.125) = 50 → d × 0.09722 = 50 → d = 50 ÷ 0.09722 = 514 km
(b) λ = v ÷ f = 8.0 ÷ 2.0 = 4.0 km
(a) 510 km (514 km)  ·  (b) 4.0 km
Keeping distance in km and speed in km/s cancels out the need to convert to metres here.
