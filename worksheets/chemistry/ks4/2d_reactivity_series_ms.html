Magnesium is the reducing agent (it gives electrons to Cu²⁺ to reduce it, while Mg itself is oxidised). Cu²⁺ is the oxidising agent.
Agents do the job to the OTHER substance: oxidising agent gets reduced; reducing agent gets oxidised.
F4
Mg atoms lose electrons to form Mg²⁺ (oxidation); H⁺ ions gain electrons to form H₂ (reduction). Both happen together, so it is a redox reaction.

G & H  Rusting and prevention

G1–G2
Tube 1: water ✓, O₂ ✓ → rusts · Tube 2: water ✓, O₂ ✗ (boiling drove out O₂; oil stops air re-entering) → no rust · Tube 3: water ✗ (CaCl₂ absorbs moisture), O₂ ✓ → no rust · Tube 4: water ✓, O₂ ✓ + salt → rusts faster. G2: water and oxygen.
H (table)
Barrier (paint on gates / grease on bike chain): stops water and oxygen reaching iron · Galvanising (zinc-coated buckets/roofs): barrier + zinc protects even when scratched · Sacrificial (Mg/Zn blocks on ships/pipes): more reactive metal oxidises instead of iron.
H1
Zinc is more reactive than iron, so zinc loses electrons (is oxidised) more easily than iron and reacts instead of the iron — sacrificial protection.
H2
Magnesium is more reactive than iron, so it reacts/corrodes in place of the steel pipe. Copper is less reactive than iron, so iron would corrode even faster instead.
Only iron (and steel) "rusts" — for other metals, say "corrodes" or "oxidises".
