Reminder: balancing
Atoms are not made or destroyed in a reaction, so there must be the same number of each atom on both sides. Balance by writing big numbers in front of formulae. Never change the small numbers — that would make a different substance. A big number multiplies everything in the formula after it, including anything in brackets: 2Ca(OH)2 has 2 Ca, 4 O and 4 H.
Worked example: hydrogen + oxygen → water
H2 + O2 → H2O   O: 2 on the left, 1 on the right ✗
H2 + O2 → 2H2O   O fixed, but now H: 2 left, 4 right ✗
2H2 + O2 → 2H2O   H 4 = 4, O 2 = 2 ✓

C  From words to symbols write a balanced equation with state symbols

State symbols: (s) solid · (l) liquid · (g) gas · (aq) aqueous, dissolved in water.   Gases made of two atoms: H2, O2, N2, Cl2. Metals and carbon are written as single atoms: Mg, Na, C.
C1. magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide (Mg, MgO)
C2. sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen (NaOH)
C3. calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide (on heating) (CaCO3, CaO)
C4. hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water (HCl, NaOH)
C5. glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (respiration) (glucose C6H12O6)
C6. glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide (fermentation) (ethanol C2H5OH)
