D  Magnesium oxide

D1. Masses
20.24 − 20.00 = 0.24; 20.40 − 20.24 = 0.16
Mg 0.24 g, O 0.16 g
D2. Formula
Mg 0.24 ÷ 24 = 0.01; O 0.16 ÷ 16 = 0.01 → 1 : 1
MgO
D3. Lid
The lid stops magnesium oxide smoke escaping (which would lose mass); lifting it lets oxygen (air) in so the magnesium can react.
D4. Constant mass
To make sure all the magnesium has reacted.
Practical AO3 (heating Mg in a crucible): lift the lid briefly to let oxygen in so all the magnesium reacts, but close it quickly so white MgO smoke does not escape (which would make the mass too low).

E1. Formula
O = 3.98 − 3.18 = 0.80 g. Cu 3.18 ÷ 63.5 = 0.050; O 0.80 ÷ 16 = 0.050 → 1 : 1
CuO
E2. Cool in the gas
So hot copper does not react with oxygen in the air and turn back into copper oxide.
E3. Burn the gas
Hydrogen (or methane) is flammable: burning it safely stops a build-up that could explode.

5 (CuSO4·5H2O)
F3. All the water gone
Heat, cool and weigh again until the mass stops changing (constant mass).
Empirical formula steps: divide mass (or %) by Aᵣ → divide all by the smallest → multiply up if you get .5 or .33. For molecular formula, divide Mᵣ by the empirical mass.
Heat to constant mass (re-heat and re-weigh until the mass stops dropping) to make sure all the water of crystallisation has been driven off.
