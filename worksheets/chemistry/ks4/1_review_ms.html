G  Structure

G1. H2S vs CaO
H2S is simple molecular: only weak forces between molecules to overcome. CaO is giant ionic: strong electrostatic attractions between many oppositely charged ions must be overcome.
G2. SiO2 (any two)
Many strong covalent bonds must be broken; there are no ions or free electrons.
High melting/boiling point; hard; does not conduct; insoluble.

H  More on electrons, bonding and moles Double Award only

H1
Group 6 · Period 3 · sulfur · acidic oxide
H2. Li and Na
Both have 1 electron in their outer shell (both in Group 1), and reactions depend on outer electrons.
H3. Argon
Its outer shell is full (2.8.8), so it does not need to gain, lose or share electrons.
H4. CaO
2 electrons transferred from Ca to O
H4. H2S
2 shared pairs; S keeps 2 non-bonding pairs
H5. Graphite electrode
Graphite has delocalised electrons that can move and carry charge; diamond has none (all 4 outer electrons are in bonds).
H6. Molten CaO
In the solid the ions are fixed in the lattice; when molten the ions are free to move and carry charge.
H7. Cl2 vs Br2
Br2 has a larger Mr (160 vs 71), so the intermolecular forces are stronger and need more energy to overcome: higher boiling point.
