Reminder: the particle model
All matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving (or vibrating). In each state the particles have a different arrangement, movement and energy. Heating gives particles more energy; changes of state happen when the particles get enough energy to overcome (or are held by) the forces of attraction between them.
Drawing tip: draw every particle the same size. Liquid particles must still touch.

H  Diffusion of gases

H1. Where the ring forms
nearer the HCl end (about 60% of the way along from the ammonia end)
H2. Why
Ammonia particles are lighter (Mr 17 vs 36.5), so they move faster and travel further in the same time. The gases meet and react nearer the HCl end.
lighter particles move faster
H3. Warmer tube
the ring forms sooner (in about the same place)
Particles move faster at a higher temperature, so they diffuse faster.
H5. Liquids vs gases
Liquid particles move more slowly and are touching, so they keep colliding with neighbours and spread out more slowly.
particles move slower / are closer, more collisions
H4. Bromine jars
Brown particles spread evenly through both jars (some air particles in the bottom jar). Particles move randomly and spread from high to low concentration: diffusion.
evenly mixed in both jars

K  Explain it with particles

K1. You can smell perfume from across a room.
Perfume particles evaporate, then move randomly and diffuse from high to low concentration through the air.
evaporation, then diffusion
K2. A puddle dries up on a sunny day, even though it never boils.
Particles at the surface with enough energy escape: evaporation. The Sun gives more particles that energy.
evaporation from the surface
K3. A syringe of air can be squashed, but a syringe of water can't.
Gas particles are far apart, so they can be pushed closer; liquid particles are already touching.
gas particles far apart, liquid touching
K4. You can pour a liquid, but you can't pour a solid.
Liquid particles can move past each other; solid particles are held in fixed positions by strong forces.
liquid particles move past each other
