Covalent Bonding

Non-metals can share a pair of electrons with another non-metal.

•Both obtain complete outer shells.

 

Strong electrostatic attractions are formed.

•The negatively charged electron pair is strongly attracted to the positively charged nuclei of the atoms involved.

Simple Molecular Structures

Atoms within a molecule are held together by very strong covalent bonds.

•Some molecules are attracted to each other through comparatively weak intermolecular forces.

 

Weak intermolecular forces mean most covalent molecules are liquids or gases at room temperature.

•As molecule size increases, intermolecular forces also increase

•So larger molecules have higher melting and boiling points

 

Intermolecular attractions (represented as a dashed line) are found between many covalent molecules, perhaps most famously in water.

Giant Covalent Structures

Giant covalent structures are similar to giant ionic lattices without charge ions

•Many non-metal atoms are strongly bonded to other non-metal atoms.

 

Lots of energy is required to break these bonds.

•very high melting and boiling points.

•do not conduct electricity (except graphite)

•insoluble in water.

Diamond

Diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon.

•They contain carbon in different structural arrangements.

 

Diamond is a giant network of carbon atoms.

•Each carbon atom is bonded to 4 other carbon atoms.

•Very hard due to strong covalent lattice

•Very high melting point - above 4000°C.

•no delocalised electrons so does not conduct electricity.

Graphite

Diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon.

•They contain carbon in different structural arrangements.

 

Graphite has delocalised electrons so can conduct electricity.

•Each carbon atom is bonded to 3 other carbon atoms.

•This creates layers of carbon atoms which can slide over one another, making graphite soft.

 

 

Diamond is made up of a network of carbon atoms, each forming 4 bonds.

Graphite is made up of sheets of carbon atoms, each forming 3 bonds.

Diamonds are often prized in jewellry, but have many applications in industry.

 

Their hardness is particularly useful for cutting and drilling.

Graphite is one of the only non-metal structures to conduct electricity. This is due to its delocalised electron which can carry charge.

C60 Fullerene

Fullerenes are hollow-shaped simple covalent molecules

•Many applications as lubricants, catalysts and as drug delivery systems.

 

E.g. C60 fullerene is a hollow ball shape with 60 carbon atoms.

•Held together by intermolecular forces.

•They are soft and can slide over each other.

•do not conduct electricity (no delocalised electrons)

•huge surface area.
