Stock Market Challenge

Trade shares, react to the news and read the graphs. Can you turn £1,000 into more?

1

What is a share?

A company can be split into millions of small pieces called shares. Buy one and you own a tiny part of that company. If the company does well, your share is usually worth more.

2

What is the stock market?

A marketplace where people buy and sell shares, like the London Stock Exchange. Prices are set by supply and demand: more buyers than sellers pushes the price up; more sellers pushes it down.

3

Why do prices change?

News. A new invention or a big contract makes people want the shares, so the price rises. Bad news makes people sell. Nobody can predict the future perfectly, so shares are a risk.

4

Reading the graph

x-axis = time (days). y-axis = share price (£). A line going up shows a rising trend. The steeper the line, the bigger the gradient and the faster the price is changing.

How to play

  1. You start with £1,000 and have 20 trading days.
  2. Pick a company, choose how many shares, then Buy or Sell.
  3. Press Next day. Prices move and news breaks. Read why it changed.
  4. Every 3 days you get a graph question. Each correct answer earns a £50 bonus.
  5. Your score is your total worth at the end: cash + the value of your shares.

All companies and news are made up.

Key words

Share – a small part of a company that you can own.
Stock market – where shares are bought and sold.
Investor – someone who buys shares hoping they grow in value.
Dividend – part of a company's profit paid to its shareholders.
Trend – the general direction a graph is moving.
Gradient – how steep a line is: change in y ÷ change in x.
Percentage change – (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
Diversification – spreading money across companies to reduce risk.
Volatile – a share whose price jumps up and down a lot.
