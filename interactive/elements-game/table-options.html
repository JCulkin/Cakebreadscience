Three ways to draw the table

All three keep the real shape of the Periodic Table, so students see what they'll see in class and in the exam. The first 20 elements are the bold, clickable ones; everything else is shown faintly so they can see where the first 20 sit. Use the buttons to see each one as it would look in the game.

Show as: Tap any of the first 20 to test clicking.

AThe whole table, first 20 lit up

The full Periodic Table (periods 1–7, without the lanthanides and actinides), like a classroom poster. Hydrogen sits above lithium. Group numbers are Edexcel style: 1, 2, 3–7, 0.

Good

  • Instantly familiar: it's the table on the wall.
  • Shows that the first 20 are only the top corner of a much bigger table.
  • Good for "Place it": students orient themselves from the whole shape.

Watch out

  • The first 20 are smaller, because most of the space goes to elements we don't use.
  • On a phone it needs sideways scrolling.

BExam data-sheet style

Modelled on the Periodic Table printed in Edexcel International GCSE papers: thin black grid, relative atomic mass at the top, symbol, name, and atomic number at the bottom of each box. Hydrogen floats on its own above the middle, and a key explains the box.

Good

  • Students practise reading the exact table they'll use in the exam, including the mass/atomic-number positions (a common mix-up).
  • Fits Atom anatomy questions: protons and neutrons straight from the box.

Watch out

  • Plainer and busier, so less eye-catching for a game.
  • Small text on a projector unless zoomed; needs sideways scrolling on phones.

CThe top of the real table, zoomed in

Only periods 1–4, but at full width with the real gap where the transition metals go. Period 4's transition metals and Ga–Kr are shown faintly. Big boxes, so it reads well from the back of the room.

Good

  • Real shape, but the first 20 get big boxes, which is best for the projector and for tapping.
  • The transition-metal gap is visible, so it matches the full table students know.

Watch out

  • Cut off at period 4, so it doesn't show the whole table.
  • Still wide: on phones the boxes get small or need scrolling.
My recommendation: C for the game rounds (real shape, big targets), with B as the table in Atom anatomy and Explore mode so students also get used to the exam version. Or pick any one to use everywhere. Two other choices to make: should hydrogen sit above lithium (A, C) or float on its own (B, as on the exam paper)? And on phones, should the table scroll sideways or shrink?
