H  Giant covalent structures

H1 [9] diamond high · hard · no  |  graphite high · soft · yes  |  C₆₀ low · soft · no
H2 [3]
  • each C atom is joined to 4 others by strong covalent bonds
  • in a giant 3D network, so atoms can't move (hard)
  • lots of energy needed to break the many strong bonds
H3 [3]
  • in graphite each C uses only 3 outer electrons in bonds
  • the 4th electron is delocalised / free to move and carry charge
  • in diamond all 4 are used in bonds → no free electrons
H4 [2]
  • weak forces between the layers
  • layers can slide over each other
H5 [2]
  • C₆₀ is made of separate molecules (simple molecular)
  • melting only overcomes weak intermolecular forces, not covalent bonds
H6 [2]
  • soft: the weak forces between layers let the layers slide
  • melting needs the strong covalent bonds within the layers to break

★ Exam-style questions

X1 [6] any six points (max 3 if only one substance is described):
  • NaCl has a giant ionic lattice
  • with strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions
  • melting NaCl means overcoming many of these strong attractions → lots of energy
  • HCl is simple molecular / made of small molecules
  • with only weak intermolecular forces between the molecules
  • the covalent bond inside each HCl molecule does not break on melting
  • so much less energy is needed to melt HCl
Answers saying HCl's covalent bonds break, or that NaCl has intermolecular forces, lose that point.
X2 (a) [3] Ca²⁺ in brackets (no outer electrons shown) · two F⁻ ions each with 8 outer electrons (7 dots + 1 cross) · brackets and charges on all three ions
(b) [3] solid: ions fixed in position · molten: ions free to move · moving ions carry the charge
X3 (a) [2] 3 shared pairs N–H · 1 lone pair on N
(b) [1] 1
(c) [2] weak intermolecular forces between molecules · little energy needed to overcome them (covalent bonds not broken)
X4 [6] any six:
  • diamond: each C bonded to 4 others
  • giant 3D network of strong covalent bonds → rigid, very hard
  • graphite: each C bonded to 3 others, in layers (hexagons)
  • weak forces between layers → layers slide → soft
  • graphite has delocalised electrons (one per C)
  • which are free to move and carry charge → conducts
  • diamond: all outer electrons in bonds → no free electrons → doesn't conduct
X5 (a) [2] chloromethane has a larger Mr / bigger molecule · so stronger intermolecular forces, more energy to overcome
(b) [3] MgCl₂ is giant ionic · strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions · far more energy needed than to overcome weak intermolecular forces

Using this mark scheme

  • Award one mark per point unless the question says otherwise. Accept any wording that gives the same chemistry.
  • Key words examiners look for: electrostatic attraction, oppositely charged ions, giant lattice, shared pair of electrons, intermolecular forces, delocalised electrons, free to move.
  • The three most common lost marks in this topic:
    • saying covalent bonds break when a simple molecular substance melts or boils;
    • saying electrons carry the current in molten or dissolved ionic compounds (it is the ions);
    • missing brackets in formulae with more than one compound ion.
  • Dot-and-cross diagrams: dots and crosses may be swapped, inner shells are not needed, and circles do not have to overlap neatly as long as the shared pairs are clearly between the two atoms.
  • After marking, go back to the Check your progress table on page 12 of the booklet and shade your confidence for each line.
