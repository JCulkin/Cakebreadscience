A  How ions form

IGCSE Edexcel Chemistry · Topic booklet
1.37 · ions formed by electron loss or gain
Name:
Key ideas
  • An ion is a charged particle made when an atom loses or gains electrons.
  • Metals lose their outer electrons → positive ions (cations).
  • Non-metals gain electrons → negative ions (anions).
  • Both end up with a full outer shell, the same electron arrangement as a noble gas.
  • The number of protons does not change. Charge = protons − electrons.

Non-metal ion names end in -ide: oxide, chloride, nitride, sulfide.
  • In a compound the charges must add up to zero.

D  Giant ionic lattices

IGCSE Edexcel Chemistry · Topic booklet
1.41–1.43 · electrostatic attraction, melting points, conductivity
Name:
Key ideas
  • Ionic bonding = strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
  • The ions form a giant lattice: a regular 3D arrangement, with attractions acting in all directions.
  • High melting and boiling points: a lot of energy is needed to overcome the many strong attractions.
  • Bigger charges (e.g. Mg²⁺ and O²⁻) → stronger attraction → higher melting point.
  • Conductivity: solid ✗ (ions fixed in place) · molten ✓ · dissolved in water ✓ (ions free to move and carry charge).
Sodium chloride lattice: small Na⁺ (+), large Cl⁻ (−)

E  Covalent bonds

IGCSE Edexcel Chemistry · Topic booklet
1.44–1.45 · shared pairs of electrons, electrostatic attraction
Name:
Key ideas
  • A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons between two atoms (usually non-metals).
  • Each atom shares electrons until it has a full outer shell.
  • The bond is the electrostatic attraction between the shared pair (negative) and the nuclei (positive) of both atoms.
  • 1 shared pair = single bond, 2 = double bond, 3 = triple bond. Pairs that are not shared are lone pairs.
Both nuclei attract the shared pair: this holds the atoms together

Key ideas: how to draw a molecule
  • Outer-shell electrons: H 1 · C 4 · N 5 · O 6 · Cl 7 (halogens 7). Hydrogen is full with 2; the others with 8.
  • Draw overlapping circles. Put each shared pair in the overlap: one dot and one cross. Add the lone pairs outside the overlap.

G  Simple molecular substances

IGCSE Edexcel Chemistry · Topic booklet
1.47–1.48 · intermolecular forces, melting points and relative molecular mass
Name:
Key ideas
  • Simple molecular substances are made of small molecules. Inside each molecule: strong covalent bonds. Between molecules: weak intermolecular forces.
  • Melting or boiling only overcomes the intermolecular forces. The covalent bonds do not break.
  • Little energy is needed → low melting and boiling points (often gases or liquids at room temperature).
  • Bigger molecules (higher relative molecular mass, Mr) → stronger intermolecular forces → higher boiling point.
  • No ions and no free electrons → they don't conduct electricity.

H  Giant covalent structures

1.49–1.51 · diamond, graphite and C₆₀ fullerene
Name:
Key ideas
  • Giant covalent substances: every atom is joined to others by strong covalent bonds in a huge network. Melting means breaking many strong covalent bonds → very high melting points.
  • Covalent substances don't usually conduct (no ions, no free electrons). Graphite is the exception.
Diamond: each C bonded to 4 others
